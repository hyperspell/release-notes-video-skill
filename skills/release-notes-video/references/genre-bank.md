# Genre bank

Expand these sketches into Suno style seeds using [Song](song.md). Choose references the audience recognizes, then make the production precise. Keep artist names out of the seed when the service rejects them. Record used directions in the private genre history described in [Genre and story](genre-and-story.md). Musical and visual ideas here are creative starting points, not claims about a product or model.

Use the actual product UI for demonstrations; period graphics can frame it without inventing another interface. Numbered genre props can be scenery, but keep release labels and recap lists unnumbered. Use original lyrics and melodies; verify rights separately for any borrowed work.

Fields: **Sound** · **World** (narrator, setting, clip look) · **Type & UI** · **Format joke** · **Fits** (what kind of week) · **Watch** (pitfalls)

---

### French-touch filter house, late-90s Paris
- **Sound:** 124 BPM, filtered disco loop, sidechained bass, vocoder harmonies, chopped guitar stabs, deadpan spoken female verses, euphoric sung chorus, crowd shouts.
- **World:** office turned club, five-metre geometric stage, 35mm late-90s music video.
- **Type & UI:** club-flyer display type, clean system-message bubbles and readable product inserts.
- **Format joke:** mundane office status messages treated as nightclub announcements.
- **Fits:** collaboration or knowledge updates with a strong before-and-after payoff.
- **Watch:** leave space for dry, intelligible update lines between the bigger sung hooks.

### Eurobeat, the drift-racing anime era (1998–2002)
- **Sound:** 155 BPM; galloping octave bass, stabbing synth brass, power-chord guitar riffs, reverb-soaked male lead with a female double, a soaring key-changed chorus. The English is slightly off-kilter and very earnest.
- **World:** a mountain pass at night. The narrator drives a hatchback drifting hairpins, with the agents as the pit crew. Clips are late-90s cel-shaded anime racing, with speed lines.
- **Type & UI:** huge slanted italic captions, a lap-timer and tachometer HUD, corner names as chips. The unread count is the lap counter.
- **Format joke:** each release is a corner on the course map; the final corner is "You're all caught up".
- **Fits:** many releases, or speed and performance.
- **Watch:** no lines lifted from famous Eurobeat songs; gesture at them. If a take drifts into generic EDM, try "Italian dance studio" production language.

### Japanese city pop, Tokyo 1984
- **Sound:** 112 BPM; major-seventh chords, slap bass, Rhodes, gated snare, chorus-drenched guitar, an alto sax solo; a breathy, glossy female vocal; a 1984 digital-era mix.
- **World:** a neon highway at 2 AM and an apartment with a skyline view. The mascot sits in a hi-fi stack. VHS and 35mm.
- **Type & UI:** cassette J-card chips, Japanese-retro poster layout, a fictional 1984 TV commercial for the product with a sung jingle tag.
- **Format joke:** the whole thing is a vintage electronics commercial ("Made for the modern office").
- **Fits:** a calm, quality-of-life week.
- **Watch:** keep the tempo from making verses sleepy. No faux-Japanese text; use real phrases or none.

### Mid-90s Eurodance (1993–1996)
- **Sound:** 135 BPM; rave piano stabs, hoover synth, offbeat hats. A deadpan male rapper delivers the release verses; a belting female diva sings the hook. Remove "rap" from the exclude list.
- **World:** a laser warehouse, early 3D CGI (chrome logos, wireframe tunnels), robot agents in oversized bomber jackets.
- **Type & UI:** rotating chrome 3D text, Windows 95 dialogs as UI frames.
- **Format joke:** the rapper reads the release notes and the diva answers "You're all caught up".
- **Fits:** lots of releases.
- **Watch:** the rap must be dry and intelligible; screen it hard.

### TRL-era boy band (1999–2001)
- **Sound:** 104 BPM; syncopated R&B-pop, staccato strings, beatbox, five-part male harmonies, a spoken bridge, a truck-driver key change.
- **World:** five robot agents as the band in matching white outfits, on a rooftop in the rain, on marionette strings, with screaming fans holding signs ("AGENT 3 ASK A HUMAN FIRST 4 ME").
- **Type & UI:** an MTV-style countdown and music-video lower-third credits, where the "Director" and "Label" fields hold the release name and the owner.
- **Format joke:** the features get request-show dedications, without numbering the release list. Each band member has a distinct personality.
- **Fits:** agent and automation releases.
- **Watch:** nothing branded MTV; build a fictional channel.

### Gilbert & Sullivan patter song (Victorian comic opera)
- **Sound:** 150 BPM patter. A pit orchestra (strings, woodwinds, timpani), a baritone rattling syllables, a chorus repeating each couplet back, a grand ritardando ending. Write an original melody with the form's precise diction and escalating patter.
- **World:** a proscenium stage with painted flats. The narrator wears a waistcoat and monocle; fictional office clerks form the chorus.
- **Type & UI:** wood-type playbill, footlights, surtitles. Product UI appears as framed portraits on the set.
- **Format joke:** "I am the very model of a modern company brain", with the patter as the release list.
- **Fits:** a week with many medium releases.
- **Watch:** patter at speed hurts intelligibility, so keep chips big and lines short. Test whether Suno gets operetta right before committing.

### 1960s spy-film title theme
- **Sound:** 92 BPM swagger; brassy big-band stabs, a twangy baritone guitar riff, sweeping strings, a belting female torch singer, a final held note.
- **World:** an iris-wipe open through the cursor, silhouettes dancing over liquid colour, a casino of dashboards. The narrator in a dinner jacket.
- **Type & UI:** title-sequence geometric sans, dossier stamps ("ONLY VISIBLE TO YOU"), agent codenames.
- **Format joke:** permissions as espionage; a licence to share.
- **Fits:** privacy and access-control releases. Use the organization's approved process for security communications.
- **Watch:** no 007 marks or gun-barrel copy; evoke them.

### Schoolhouse educational cartoon (1973)
- **Sound:** 110 BPM; jazzy piano, upright bass, brushed drums, a folksy male lead, a kids' chorus on the hook.
- **World:** limited-animation cartoon with flat colours and thick outlines. A little release note walks from a merged PR to your Inbox.
- **Type & UI:** hand-lettered titles, chalkboard diagrams; the product UI drawn as a classroom filmstrip.
- **Format joke:** "I'm just a note" style explainer: how a feature works, step by step.
- **Fits:** one release that needs explaining (the action should be immediately understandable).
- **Watch:** structure only, no lyrics from the original.

### Bardcore (medieval covers of modern pop)
- **Sound:** 96 BPM in 6/8; lute, recorder, crumhorn, frame drum, hurdy-gurdy, a clear female folk vocal using "thee" and "thine".
- **World:** illuminated-manuscript animation, a Bayeux-tapestry Slack embroidered as a chat, monks copying the changelog by candlelight, marginalia snails.
- **Type & UI:** blackletter titles with illuminated drop caps; chips as wax-sealed scrolls; captions in a readable book serif.
- **Format joke:** medieval words for modern features ("thy DMs remain thine own").
- **Fits:** memory, archive or knowledge releases.
- **Watch:** blackletter is unreadable as captions; use it only for titles.

### MySpace-era emo pop-punk (2005)
- **Sound:** 170 BPM; palm-muted power chords, nasal male lead, gang vocals, a screamed bridge, a breakdown, a ringing final chord.
- **World:** a basement show, a mall food court, robot agents with side-swept fringes.
- **Type & UI:** a fictional MySpace-style profile frames actual product captures: Top 8 integrations, mood "caught up", glitter titles and an autoplaying song-player prop.
- **Format joke:** the Top 8 and away messages; the screamed bridge is the sincere turn.
- **Fits:** integrations and connectors.
- **Watch:** caption the screams; release lines go in sung verses.

### Nu-metal (2001)
- **Sound:** 95 BPM; drop-D chug, turntable scratches, a deadpan rapped verse against a sung or screamed chorus, quiet-loud dynamics.
- **World:** a warehouse lit in sodium orange, frosted tips, fish-eye lens, early-CGI menace.
- **Type & UI:** distressed industrial stencil; UI glitching in and out.
- **Format joke:** maximal angst about tiny workplace pain ("I scrolled so far up").
- **Fits:** pain-point releases such as search or finding decisions.
- **Watch:** shares territory with emo; one per quarter.

### German robot pop, minimal synth (1981)
- **Sound:** 120 BPM; sequenced synth bass, vocoder lead, electronic drums, calculator bleeps, monotone male speech, lots of space.
- **World:** four mannequin agents in red shirts at consoles, a Bauhaus grid, a 1981 TV studio.
- **Type & UI:** constructivist red, black and white; seven-segment numbers; the product UI on a green phosphor monitor.

### Drift phonk + "sigma edit"
- **Sound:** 130 BPM; cowbell melody, distorted 808, chopped pitched-down vocal chants. The release lines are a dry female spoken voice over the beat, since phonk vocals don't carry words.
- **World:** grayscale slow-motion, film grain, the narrator in sunglasses, marble statues, night drives.
- **Type & UI:** big condensed white captions popping word by word; motivational quote cards.
- **Format joke:** subvert the sigma edit. "The agent asked a human first. Then made a dramatic entrance."
- **Fits:** guardrails, approvals and reliability.
- **Watch:** keep it short and the words clear; no real-person sigma icons.

### Fourth-generation K-pop girl group (Y2K and Jersey club, 2022)
- **Sound:** 130 BPM with Jersey-club kicks; bubblegum synths, bright breathy group vocals, a rap verse, a dance break, an English hook.
- **World:** the dance-practice video (mirror wall, locked-off camera, sweatsuits), member fancams of each agent, comeback teaser stills.
- **Type & UI:** member-name lower thirds, fancam overlays with heart counts, Y2K stickers, album concept-photo layouts.
- **Format joke:** "Agent 3 focus cam". Each release is a member with its own fancam.
- **Fits:** a multi-release week.
- **Watch:** English lyrics only; the joke is the comeback-marketing machine, never the people or the language.

### Sea shanty (the 2021 revival)

- **World:** a 19th-century ship crewed by agents, the mascot as the ship's lantern, a storm.
- **Type & UI:** ship's-log handwriting, nautical charts; Slack threads as call and response.

- **Fits:** collaboration and team releases.

### Polka medley
- **Sound:** 126 BPM oom-pah; accordion, tuba, clarinet, a hyperactive male lead; a medley with hard transitions between features.
- **World:** a beer-hall office party; the agents in lederhosen-adjacent uniforms (affectionate).
- **Type & UI:** circus-poster type, a medley track list.
- **Format joke:** every release gets one verse, then a hard cut to the next. The genre is built for lists.
- **Fits:** a week with many small-to-medium releases.
- **Watch:** check medley transitions in Suno; a Cover might be needed.
